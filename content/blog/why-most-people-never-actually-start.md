title: "Why most people never actually start"
date: "2026-08-04"
excerpt: "It's not a lack of information. There's more of that than ever. It's that information was never the missing piece."
---

Open any platform and you'll find thousands of hours of content on how to start a business. Sales frameworks, marketing playbooks, tool comparisons, case studies, entire courses on entrepreneurship. None of it is hard to find anymore.

And yet most people who want to start a business never do. Not because they didn't watch enough videos — usually because they watched too many, and never got past the research phase.

**The questions never actually go away.** What should you sell? Where do you find customers? What do you say to them? Which tools do you need? How do you actually deliver the service? Watching someone else answer these questions on a screen doesn't answer them for your specific situation. So people keep watching, hoping the next video will be the one that finally makes it click.

**Information doesn't remove the first-time cost.** The first sales call you make, the first contract you send, the first CRM you set up — all of that has a cost in time and mistakes, whether you learned the theory first or not. Reading about how to hold a sales conversation and being ready to hold one are two different skills, and only one of them gets built by consuming more content.

**Nobody tells you what "done researching" looks like.** There's no finish line to cross that says you now know enough. So the research phase quietly becomes the business — permanently in preparation, never in execution.

This is the specific gap BIAB is built to close: not more information, but the infrastructure that turns "I should start a business" into a working setup you can act on this week.
